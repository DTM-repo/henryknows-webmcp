The one-online-class rule, explained

By an active DSO at a large public university · Updated July 10, 2026

Short answer: no more than one class or three credits of online coursework per term can count toward your full course of study — and if you're down to a single final class, it can't be online at all. 8 CFR 214.2(f)(6)(i)(G)

The rule counts toward the minimum, not against your schedule

This is the piece that unlocks most confusion: the limit applies to the credits that satisfy your full course of study requirement — typically 12 undergraduate or 9 graduate credits. Online classes beyond that minimum are your business. Concretely, for an undergraduate needing 12 credits:

  • 9 in-person + 3 online = 12 counted credits. Compliant.
  • 6 in-person + 6 online = 9 counted credits (only one online class counts). Out of status.
  • 12 in-person + 6 online = 12 counted credits plus electives. Compliant — the extra online classes never enter the math.

What "online" means here

The regulation reaches classes that don't require your physical attendance for classes, exams, or other purposes integral to completing the class. A hybrid course with required in-person sessions generally counts as in-person; a course that's technically "on campus" but never requires you in a room generally doesn't. The registrar's course coding usually decides how your school reports it — when your reading of a syllabus and the code disagree, that's a DSO conversation before enrollment, not after.

The final-semester trap: needing only one class to graduate, and that class is online. This exact scenario shows up every spring. The regulation is explicit — the last class cannot be online-only — and the fixes all require lead time: a different section, an added in-person course, or a re-planned final term.

Why this rule gets students in trouble quietly

Nothing errors out when you register. The schedule looks full, the tuition bill is real, and SEVIS registration happens at the start of term — the mismatch surfaces later, when someone audits what counted. By then you've spent a semester below a full course of study without knowing it. The five-minute prevention: before each registration, count only your in-person credits plus one online class, and check that number against your program's full-time minimum.

FAQ

Can I take two online classes in one semester?

You can enroll in as many as you like — but only one (or 3 credits) counts toward your required full course of study. Twelve required credits with two 3-credit online classes means only 9 counted credits: out of status. Fifteen credits enrolled with twelve in-person and three online: fine.

Does a hybrid class count as online?

A class with a required physical-attendance component is generally not treated as online/distance education for this rule. The test is whether the class requires your physical presence — check how the registrar codes it, and ask your DSO before assuming.

My last remaining class is only offered online. What are my options?

The final course of a program cannot be online-only. Real options from casework: add a second (in-person) course so the online one isn’t your only enrollment, find an in-person or hybrid section, or talk to your DSO about the program-end timeline. Decide before the term starts, not after.
