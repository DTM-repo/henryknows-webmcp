# What the duration-of-status final rule changed, effective September 15, 2026

**This document controls over any pre-rule text in this knowledge base, and it is
date-sensitive — check today's date before answering.** The final rule was published
July 17, 2026 and takes effect **September 15, 2026**.

- **Before September 15, 2026:** the OLD rules (the eCFR snapshots, Policy Manual
  text) are still current law — for example, the 60-day OPT filing window still
  applies to applications USCIS receives through September 14, 2026, and
  applications pending on September 15, 2026 remain valid (8 CFR 214.1(m)(2)).
  Answer with current law AND flag what changes on September 15, since deadlines
  straddling the date are exactly where students get hurt. A student with an open
  old-rule window should file before September 15 rather than assume extra days.
- **On or after September 15, 2026:** the new rules below control. State them as
  current law and mention the old rule only as history.

Where the corpus snapshots conflict with this document or the final rule text
(kb/corpus/fr-2026-14439-ds-final-rule.md), the final rule controls for the period
on or after its effective date.

## Duration of status ended — fixed-period admission replaced it

- F-1 students admitted after September 15, 2026 no longer receive duration of status
  (D/S). They are admitted for the length of the I-20 program, up to a maximum of
  **4 years measured from the I-20 program start date** (not the entry date), plus a
  30-day departure period reflected on the I-94. 8 CFR 214.1(a)(4), 214.2(f)(5)(i).
- Exceptions: English language training programs capped at **24 months**; public high
  school capped at **12 months** aggregate; an F-2's stay may not exceed the F-1's.
  8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(i)(A)–(E).
- Entry is allowed up to **30 days before** the I-20 program start date; those days do
  not count against the 4-year maximum.

## Transition rule for students who already had D/S

- Students physically in the U.S. on September 15, 2026, **maintaining F-1 status**,
  who were **admitted for duration of status**, keep old-rules treatment while they
  remain: authorized stay runs to the LATER of the I-20 program end date or EAD
  expiration in place that day, **capped at September 15, 2030**, plus a 60-day
  departure period (outer date November 14, 2030). 8 CFR 214.1(m)(1).
- **Travel converts the admission**: leaving and re-entering after September 15, 2026
  ends transition treatment — the student is readmitted with a fixed-date I-94
  computed from the I-20 program dates, and the departure period becomes 30 days.
- Students whose program or OPT ended before September 15, 2026 had only the
  remainder of their old 60-day period.

## Extensions of stay are now required and go through USCIS

- The old rule that a D/S student "is not required to apply for extension of stay"
  **stops being true on September 15, 2026**. Staying beyond a fixed period (or beyond the transition cap)
  requires **Form I-539** filed with USCIS: DSO-endorsed I-20 showing the new program
  end date, evidence of funds, fee (**$420 online / $470 on paper**, USCIS G-1055),
  possible biometrics. 8 CFR 214.2(f)(7).
- USCIS must **receive** the I-539 before the authorized stay ends, including the
  30-day departure period — but filing during the 30-day period limits the student to
  study only, with **no employment or practical training** while it is pending.
  8 CFR 214.2(f)(7)(iii)(B).
- A timely-filed extension gives a period of authorized stay while pending, and
  existing on-campus, CPT, and severe-economic-hardship employment automatically
  extends up to **240 days** from the I-94 end date (or until the DSO-authorized CPT
  end date, whichever is earlier). 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(viii).
- Extensions may be granted for up to 4 years at a time. Approval is not guaranteed.

## OPT filing window shortened: 60 days became 30

- As of September 15, 2026, the post-completion OPT Form I-765 must reach USCIS
  **no later than 30 days after the program end date** (window: 90 days before to 30 days after). The boundary is the
  receipt date: applications USCIS receives before September 15, 2026 fall under the
  old 60-day window, and applications pending on that date remain valid
  (8 CFR 214.1(m)(2)). Amended
  8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(B)(2).
- The requested OPT start date may be **no more than 30 days** after the program end
  date (was 60). Amended 8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(D).
- Unchanged: the I-765 must still reach USCIS within 30 days of the DSO's SEVIS
  recommendation. 8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(A).

## March 18, 2027 transition carve-out (avoiding the I-539 for OPT)

- A transition student recommended for post-completion OPT who files the I-765
  (with fee) **before their period of admission expires (including the 60-day
  departure period) and on or before March 18, 2027** does not need to file an I-539
  for that OPT period. STEM OPT: file before the current OPT EAD expires and by
  March 18, 2027. 8 CFR 214.1(m)(1)(i).
- If approved, stay runs to the EAD end date + 60 days. **Departing the U.S. before
  filing forfeits the carve-out** — after a fixed-period readmission, both the I-765
  and an I-539 are required. 8 CFR 214.1(m)(1)(ii).
- The carve-out removes only the I-539 requirement; it does **not** extend the 30-day
  OPT filing window. DHS may extend the March 18, 2027 date in 6-month increments by
  Federal Register notice. 8 CFR 214.1(m)(1)(iii).

## Grace/departure periods now depend on how the student was admitted

| Situation | Departure period |
|---|---|
| Transition student (D/S, in U.S. 9/15/2026, never left since) | 60 days |
| Fixed-period admission (anyone admitted after 9/15/2026) | 30 days, built into the I-94 |
| Early completion of program | 30 days from actual end |
| Authorized withdrawal (DSO) | 15 days |
| Failure to maintain status | none — depart immediately |

8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(v). Never add a second 30-day period on top of an actual I-94 date —
the fixed-period I-94 already includes it.

## Travel and pending applications

- Re-entry with a **pending I-539**: admitted for the unexpired balance of the prior
  period + 30 days; the I-539 is NOT abandoned if the student seeks admission for
  that balance — but re-entering on an I-20 showing a program end date beyond the
  previously authorized period **may be treated as abandonment**. 8 CFR 214.1(c)(8).
- A pending **I-765 is not abandoned** by travel; carry the I-797C receipt notice.
- A pending **change of status IS abandoned** on departure. 8 CFR 248.1(f).
- Automatic visa revalidation continues, but admission is only for the unexpired
  previously authorized period.

## School transfers and program changes (new restrictions)

- **Graduate students**: may not change educational objectives (major or level) at
  any point during a program; may not transfer during a program unless SEVP
  authorizes an exception for extenuating circumstances. 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii)(A).
- **Undergraduate and below**: no transfer or change of educational objectives within
  the first academic year, absent an SEVP exception.
- **Same-or-lower level bar**: a student who completes a program AFTER September 15,
  2026 may not get F-1 status for a program at the same or a lower educational level.
  Programs completed before that date do not count. 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii)(C).
- DHS may delay or suspend these transfer/program-change provisions until
  September 14, 2028; as of August 16, 2026 no delay has been announced.
  8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii)(E).

## What did NOT change

- CPT eligibility and DSO authorization (including Day-1 CPT) — unchanged, though
  CPT continuation during a pending extension is bounded by the 240-day rule above.
- Cap-gap: preserved — a timely-filed cap-subject H-1B change of status extends F-1
  status and (c)(3)(i)(B)/(C) work authorization to April 1 of the fiscal year or the
  petition's start date, whichever is earlier. 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(vii).
- The 30-days-before-SEVIS-recommendation I-765 rule, the 90-day pre-completion
  filing opening, and unemployment-day limits on OPT.
