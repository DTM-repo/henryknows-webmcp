# CPT Authorization — SEVP Broadcast Message 2608-01 (DSO Liability Reminder, Increased Scrutiny)

**Status: SEVP Broadcast Message, dated August 12, 2026 (BCM 2608-01). Guidance, NOT a rule.**
Addressed to PDSOs and DSOs at SEVP-certified schools. The message carries SEVP's standard disclaimer: it "is not a substitute for applicable legal requirements, nor is it itself a rule or a final action by SEVP" and creates no enforceable rights. The binding regulation on curricular practical training (CPT) remains 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i). What the broadcast changes is enforcement posture, not law.

## What SEVP announced

- SEVP states it "has observed a rise in CPT authorizations that do not meet regulatory requirements, specifically those not constituting an integral part of an established curriculum," and is **increasing its scrutiny of CPT approvals**.
- SEVP reserves the right to **request documentation and other evidence from DSOs** to determine whether CPT is "a core and essential part of the student's studies, without which, they would be unable to complete their degree."
- SEVP acknowledges that "the school, not the U.S. government, defines what is and is not required to complete a degree," but says it is committed to identifying and investigating schools that attempt to undermine or purposely bypass federal regulations or oversight.
- Schools suspected of "abusing CPT to create work opportunities rather than academic studies" may face action from SEVP and **Homeland Security Investigations (HSI)**, up to **withdrawal of the school's SEVP certification**.

## The liability framing (what's aimed at individual DSOs)

- When a DSO signs or re-signs the Form I-20 to authorize CPT, the DSO is **certifying under penalty of perjury** that all information is true and correct to the best of their knowledge — including that CPT is required or otherwise integral to the student's curriculum.
- Improper CPT authorization "may subject **both the institution and individual DSO** to administrative or legal consequences," including withdrawal of SEVP certification and "potential penalties for providing false information or misrepresenting the nature of the training."
- The broadcast also invokes the student's own attestation: F-1 students attest under penalty of perjury that they are in the U.S. "solely for the purpose of pursuing a full program of study."

## Actions SEVP requests of DSOs

1. Carefully review all CPT requests and the institution's current academic requirements across all fields of study.
2. Ensure CPT is only authorized when it is truly integral to the program. The broadcast's formulation: "Only authorize CPT if its absence would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility and if the work associated with CPT is required for all students pursuing that degree."
3. Maintain thorough records supporting the curricular necessity of each CPT authorization.
4. Consult your SEVP field representative with questions about a specific case.

Suspected employment or practical-training fraud can be reported to SEVPFraud@hsi.dhs.gov. Questions about the message go to the SEVP Response Center: 703-603-3400 or 1-800-892-4829, SEVP@ice.dhs.gov (M–F, 8 a.m.–6 p.m. ET).

## How the broadcast's standard compares to the regulation

Be precise about this when answering — the two formulations are not identical.

- **The regulation (8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i)):** a DSO may authorize CPT "that is an integral part of an established curriculum," defined as "alternative work/study, internship, cooperative education or any other type of required internship or practicum that is offered by sponsoring employers through cooperative agreements with the school," and it must be "directly related to the student's major area of study."
- **The broadcast's requested standard:** authorize only if the training's "absence would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility" and the work is "required for all students pursuing that degree."
- The broadcast's "impossibility / required for all students" language is **stricter than the regulatory text** and appears in the "actions requested" section of a guidance document that disclaims the force of law. Long-standing practice at many schools treats for-credit internship courses, cooperative education, and similar curricular structures as qualifying CPT even where a specific internship is not mandatory for every student in the degree. The broadcast does not amend the regulation — but it does state how SEVP intends to scrutinize authorizations, and a DSO whose file cannot connect the CPT to the established curriculum is exposed under either formulation.
- The safe advising line: CPT "cannot be authorized solely for the purpose of employment or for experiences that are not required or otherwise formally recognized as part of the academic program" (broadcast's words, consistent with the regulation).

## Unchanged regulatory mechanics (for context; all from 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)–(f)(10)(i))

- CPT requires DSO authorization in SEVIS (employer, location, full-time/part-time, start and end dates) and a signed, dated Form I-20 returned to the student **before** employment begins.
- Generally available only after one full academic year of enrollment; the regulation provides an exception for "students enrolled in graduate studies that require immediate participation in curricular practical training." Programs relying on that exception (including anything marketed as "day-1 CPT") are squarely in the category this broadcast targets — the exception exists, but the "required" element must be genuine and documentable.
- Students who receive **one year or more of full-time CPT** lose eligibility for post-completion practical training (the regulatory text says "post-completion academic training"; it is universally applied to post-completion OPT). Part-time CPT does not trigger this.

## Advising implications (DSO framing)

- **Documentation is the whole game.** For each CPT authorization, the file should show the curricular hook: the course registration or degree-audit entry, the catalog or syllabus language establishing the internship/practicum as part of the curriculum, the cooperative agreement or employer relationship, and the tie between the work and the student's major. SEVP has now said explicitly it may ask for this evidence.
- **Audit existing authorizations, not just new requests.** SEVP's requested action #1 is a review of "all CPT requests and your institution's current academic requirements across all fields of study" — a look backward, not just a gate going forward.
- **The risk runs to the individual DSO,** not only the school. This is one of the few SEVP communications that expressly frames personal liability around the I-20 certification.
- A student asking "can I get CPT for this job/internship?" should be answered curriculum-first: the question is not whether the work is related to the major (necessary but not sufficient) but whether it is part of the established curriculum. An offer letter alone never establishes CPT eligibility.
- Schools with CPT-heavy program designs (immediate-participation graduate programs, high CPT volume relative to enrollment) should expect to be the first recipients of documentation requests and should involve their SEVP field representative proactively.

## Citation hygiene (important for answering questions)

- **8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i)** — the binding CPT regulation. Cite this for what CPT *is* and what a DSO *may* authorize.
- **SEVP Broadcast Message 2608-01 (Aug. 12, 2026), "Reminder of Liability for DSOs Regarding CPT Authorization"** — cite this for the increased-scrutiny posture, the documentation warning, and the liability framing. Do not cite it as a rule or as amending the regulation; by its own terms it is neither.
- Do not present the broadcast's "impossibility / required for all students" phrasing as the regulatory standard. Attribute it to the broadcast; attribute "integral part of an established curriculum" to the regulation.
